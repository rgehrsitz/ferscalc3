## Scenarios Compared

### Scenario 1: TSP 4% Rule
- **Strategy**: Traditional 4% withdrawal, inflation-adjusted
- **Year 1 Withdrawal**: $80,000 ($2M × 4%)
- **Monthly**: ~$6,667
- **Key Feature**: Balance remains invested, can grow with markets
- **Risk**: Market volatility, potential to run out of money

### Scenario 2: F&G Fixed Annuity (Actual Quote)
- **Strategy**: Convert entire $2M to F&G annuity
- **Annual Payment**: $123,204 (guaranteed for life)
- **Monthly**: $10,267 (guaranteed)
- **Key Feature**: No market risk, guaranteed lifetime income
- **Tradeoff**: No access to principal, no growth potential

### Scenario 3: TSP 5% Rule
- **Strategy**: 5% withdrawal rate (higher than traditional 4%)
- **Year 1 Withdrawal**: $100,000 ($2M × 5%)
- **Monthly**: ~$8,333
- **Note**: Higher withdrawal rate = faster depletion risk

### Scenario 4: TSP 6% Rule
- **Strategy**: 6% withdrawal rate (close to F&G payout)
- **Year 1 Withdrawal**: $120,000 ($2M × 6%)
- **Monthly**: ~$10,000 (similar to annuity)
- **Risk**: Very high depletion risk if markets underperform

### The F&G Annuity Advantage

**Immediate Income Boost:**
- F&G pays **$43,204 more** per year than 4% rule
- That's **54% more income** in Year 1
- Extra **$3,600/month** in guaranteed income

**Why F&G Can Pay More:**
1. **No principal preservation** - They're paying back your own money plus interest
2. **Mortality pooling** - Those who die early subsidize those who live long
3. **Professional investment** - Insurance company invests the $2M
4. **Guaranteed period risk** - You might not live to collect all payments

### The TSP Withdrawal Advantage

**Flexibility & Legacy:**
- Keep access to your $2M principal
- Can increase/decrease withdrawals as needed
- Leave money to heirs
- Potential for account growth if markets do well

**Growth Potential Example (5% market returns):**
- 4% Rule: TSP balance can last 30+ years and still have ~$2M left
- 6% Rule: TSP depletes in ~20-25 years depending on returns
- Annuity: $0 principal remaining (belongs to insurance company)

## Tax Implications

**Important:** Both strategies are taxed the same way!
- TSP withdrawals from Traditional TSP = ordinary income
- Annuity payments from TSP rollover = ordinary income
- Federal tax, PA state tax, IRMAA surcharges all apply equally
- **No tax advantage** to either approach

## Understanding Death Benefits vs Guaranteed Period

**Important Clarification:**

The F&G annuity has TWO separate features that are often confused:

### 1. Death Benefit (What Heirs Get)
- **When you die**, beneficiaries receive the **remaining account value as a lump sum**
- Source: F&G Safe Income Advantage brochure states: "Your account value is paid as a lump sum death benefit to the beneficiary or beneficiaries you name in your contract"
- Account value = original $2M premium minus cumulative withdrawals, plus any credited interest
- Example: If you die in year 10 after receiving $1,232,040 in payments ($123,204 × 10), remaining account value goes to heirs
- **Key point**: The higher the payout rate (6.16%), the faster account value depletes, leaving less for heirs

### 2. Ten-Year Guaranteed Period (Income Protection)
- Ensures **minimum 10 years of income payments** to you and/or beneficiaries combined
- If you die in year 5, beneficiaries continue receiving $10,267/month for remaining 5 years
- If you live past year 10, payments continue for your lifetime (no maximum)
- This protects against "premature death" where you'd only receive a few years of payments

### 3. IRS 10-Year Rule (Tax Treatment)
- **This is what your advisor was referring to**
- When non-spouse beneficiaries inherit retirement accounts (TSP or annuity death benefits), they can spread withdrawals over 10 years
- This provides tax benefits by avoiding a single large tax hit
- **Applies equally** to TSP inheritance and annuity death benefits
- Not unique to annuities - it's an IRS rule for inherited retirement accounts

**Bottom Line on Legacy:**
- TSP 4% rule: After 20 years, likely still $2M+ for heirs (account grows while withdrawing conservatively)
- F&G annuity: After 20 years, account value significantly depleted (6.16% withdrawals exceed growth)
- Both have same IRS 10-year distribution option for tax purposes
- True difference: TSP preserves principal better due to lower withdrawal rate and continued market growth
